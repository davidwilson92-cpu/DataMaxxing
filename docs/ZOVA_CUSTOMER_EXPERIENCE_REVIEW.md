# Zova customer experience and brand review — 14 September 2026

## Verdict

Zova is not yet an exceptional customer experience or demonstrably industry-leading. It has a recognisable identity and a stronger technical foundation. Its current interaction design still resembles a general AI chat product with publishing controls added around it. The next increment needs a deliberate product-design pass around the customer's work.

The previous 7/10 UX scores gave substantial weight to repaired functionality and reachable controls. This deeper assessment places more weight on clarity, effort, distinctiveness and fulfilment of the brand promise. It revises those judgements; passing 72 technical tests does not demonstrate customer delight.

| Dimension | Current local candidate /10 | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Visual continuity with existing Zova identity | 7 | Purple, near-black, logo, typography and orbit imagery carry through |
| Brand promise reflected in product behaviour | 4 | Understanding, personal voice and continuous learning are promised more strongly than demonstrated |
| Customer journey and first value | 5 | Value is delayed by setup; first useful result is not clearly orchestrated |
| General navigation and task clarity | 6 | Areas are reachable, but naming and hierarchy mix chats, drafts, posts and settings |
| Studio creation and editing experience | 6 | Functional and safer; the document lacks a stable, generous workspace |
| Review and publishing experience | 6 | Approval integrity is stronger; requirements and visual previews remain insufficiently integrated |
| Mobile interaction quality | 5 | Controls are restored but crowd the canvas; reachability is not comfort |
| Distinctiveness / reason to choose Zova | 4 | The central promise has potential, but few interactions demonstrate it uniquely |

These are expert-review judgements, not measured customer satisfaction. The product has not regressed; this is a stricter and more detailed assessment of the same local candidate.

## Scope

Read the existing templates, interaction code and authoritative handoff. Revisited the current local landing page, empty and populated Studio, four-platform editing, publication preflight and Account; inspected desktop and 390×844 mobile views. The local account and output were synthetic, and providers/model responses were mocked. The repeated synthetic text is not evidence of real generation quality. No application changes or production actions were made during this review. No standalone brand standards document was found in the accessible workspace; brand alignment is assessed against Zova's existing identity and stated positioning.

## 1. First impression: strong words, insufficient demonstration

“One idea. Every social. Still you.” is the strongest existing brand asset after the mark. It gives Zova a clear job: preserve meaning and voice while adapting expression. Keep that organising idea, while making supported platforms and actual capabilities explicit.

The landing page describes four adaptations without showing an actual source idea and its outputs. Customers must imagine whether the result will be useful. At the inspected desktop size, the orbit/central mark competes behind the headline while much of the right side is unused. It creates atmosphere without helping a purchase or signup decision.

The CTA “Start with an idea” opens signup, not an idea-entry experience. The later CTA similarly asks for an idea and sends the customer to registration. That is a small but consequential promise mismatch. The site also makes learning/performance claims that are stronger than the saved-profile and sampled-analytics behaviour demonstrated in the application.

Direction: show a real, labelled example of one idea becoming meaningfully different platform outputs. Let the first action capture an idea and carry it through registration, or label registration honestly. Clearly explain the intended customer, current testing offer, what Zova produces, supported publishing modes and where customer approval remains required. Do not invent testimonials or evidence of success.

## 2. Activation: demonstrate value before demanding configuration

The current journey is signup → social connection choices → writing-style step → Studio. Connection is optional, which is good, but the customer still encounters setup and an optional guidance form before seeing their first useful draft. The onboarding copy can say learning has started whenever there is a connection, whereas Account can truthfully say the voice profile is not learned yet. The actual scan result should determine both messages.

“No questionnaires” is difficult to reconcile with an onboarding guidance box followed by a larger preferences form. Optional forms are not inherently bad; the promise should match how much effort customers need to supply.

Proposed journey: bring an idea → make a useful first version → refine one meaningful choice → show why each selected platform differs → connect the destination when needed → review and confirm. Preserve both Instagram routes and explain the choice where relevant. Existing connected customers should bypass setup and resume their latest useful work.

The first success should be tangible: a draft the customer recognises as theirs. Reaching Studio or finishing account setup is not that success.

## 3. Studio: make the customer's post the stable centre

Three competing areas dominate desktop Studio: a wide navigation rail, the conversation/editor, and a visually heavy social-pulse panel. The pulse panel occupies substantial space even when it contains only unavailable metrics. The post should receive that space while the customer is composing.

The editor is embedded in the scrolling conversation. In the inspected populated state, scrolling to the newest content left the platform tabs and upper draft outside the visible area. The customer must keep finding the document they are editing. On mobile, the persistent composer occupies a large proportion of the screen; the review actions sit near its edge and require careful scrolling.

The composer exposes media, source link, X thread length, four platform selections, send, save and reload controls together. Several are irrelevant before the customer has chosen a task. It also puts analytics questions and content creation into the same starting instruction. Flexibility is useful, but the initial choice is under-designed.

Direction: use a persistent working canvas for one named idea, with platform versions belonging to it. Keep conversation available as the editing assistant. Show creation context while creating, review context while reviewing, and performance context when analysing. On a phone, deliberately switch between chat, editor and preview so the keyboard/composer does not compete with the document.

A stable canvas can still feel conversational. It should let the customer point at a passage, request a change, compare the change and undo it without losing their place.

## 4. Platform adaptation must be visible

Current platform versions are mostly text fields with labels, character limits and generic refinement buttons such as Shorter, More serious, More playful and More data-led. Those actions do not demonstrate a social editor's understanding of a particular post. “More data-led” is especially unhelpful when the customer has supplied no data.

There is no strong presentation of what changed across channels or why. A customer should understand whether the output is a caption, thread, video script, concept or completed publishing asset. Instagram/TikTok copy is not the same thing as a visual story or finished video.

Direction: present format-specific previews with explicit limits on their accuracy. Show a brief explanation of meaningful adaptation: the X version leads with the opinion; Instagram anchors the caption to the selected visual; a video script separates hook, scenes and caption if scripting is a supported capability. Offer contextual edits such as “Lead with the launch date” or “Keep the personal anecdote,” grounded in the actual draft.

A comparison view and reversible edit history would make the customer feel in control. Never silently invent facts to satisfy an edit suggestion.

## 5. Personal voice should be a product experience

The voice profile is now editable, which improves control. It is still presented mainly as configuration fields inside Account. A first-time profile can display “Scan again” before a successful scan. “Extra guidance” and “Things to avoid” expose overlapping concepts in different places.

The promise “Still you” needs visible proof: the examples used, a concise explanation of the inferred style and a draft that demonstrates it. The customer should be able to say a result sounds wrong and correct a specific rule. A proposed preference should only become a lasting preference when its scope is clear.

Direction: make voice review part of creation. Show a small set of editable observations, each with an example and provenance. Distinguish creator-authored guidance, inferred preferences and temporary instructions for this post. Replace vague continuous-learning language with verified status and actual feedback mechanisms.

## 6. Trust: requirements must appear before the final click

In the local scenario, only X was connected, but all four platforms were initially selected. Four draft versions were generated, and clicking Publish then produced a message directing the customer to Account to connect the other platforms. Drafting for an unconnected platform should remain possible, but that should be a clear choice. The customer should know the difference between “draft here” and “ready to send here” throughout the workflow.

The server now binds approval to exact content/account/settings. That is essential infrastructure. The customer-facing review still needs a composed summary: exact destination identity, realistic text/media preview, time and timezone, outstanding requirements, and a clear primary action. Let the customer publish ready platforms while intentionally keeping others as drafts.

Raw statuses such as pending and unknown need plain explanations, ownership of the next step and safe recovery actions. A save conflict should provide a comparison or copy/recovery path; telling a customer to open the saved version asks them to reason about the storage model.

Likewise, “Save now” and “Open saved version” should not be permanent peers of the creative controls in the normal state. Keep save status quiet during success and reveal explicit recovery controls when needed.

## 7. Brand: consistent colours, inconsistent service

Keep the existing Z mark, purple/ink palette, confident headline voice and the idea of one source leading to several expressions. Use the orbit sparingly where it helps express that idea. Inside the working product, let customer content carry most of the visual weight.

The mixture of glowing CTA buttons, repeated orbit illustrations, rounded cards, small uppercase labels and pill controls risks looking like a familiar AI-product template. The issue is repetition without a task-specific purpose. Platform marks are also rendered inconsistently across the product: SVG marks, letter abbreviations and text symbols appear in different contexts.

Typography needs a deliberate hierarchy for reading and editing. Secondary text and metadata are still very small in several inherited styles. New recovery controls look visually appended rather than part of one coherent component family. Account repeats a top-level title and a large promotional hero before practical settings.

Brand consistency should include behaviour. A capable Zova should anticipate a missing image, retain the customer's place, explain what it understood and ask only for information needed to proceed. That conduct would make the branding credible.

## 8. Returning customers need an ongoing workflow

The default welcome remains “What are we working on?” even for returning users. Recent chats/drafts exist, but there is no strong continuation journey: resume the latest idea, review what is scheduled, resolve a failed destination, or turn a demonstrated result into the next draft.

Direction: retain a simple starting surface that changes with real state. Show a useful next action and a small number of relevant items. Do not replace the current clutter with a larger dashboard. Distinguish first-time, returning, disconnected, waiting-for-results and recovering-from-failure states.

## Industry reference points

These are documentation comparisons, not hands-on comparative usability tests or proof that another product is superior overall.

- Buffer documents per-network customisation and channel previews, plus quicker channel selection and grouped cross-channel posts. Such features make platform-aware composition a baseline expectation: https://support.buffer.com/article/642-scheduling-posts and https://buffer.com/changelog/post-composer-improvements
- Planable documents version restoration, approval controls and locking approved content. Reliable review and recovery are established category capabilities: https://help.planable.io/hc/en-us/articles/21715323068444-Getting-started-with-Planable
- Canva presents brand assets, voice and guidelines as shared context for creation. A brand profile alone is not a unique AI proposition: https://www.canva.com/business/features/brand/

Zova's strongest proposed differentiator is helping an individual creator turn a rough intention into platform-appropriate work that still sounds like them, with visibly low effort and precise control. Validate that proposition with actual target customers; do not assume it has been achieved because the interface uses chat.

## Design priorities and evidence required

1. Resolve the promise-to-product mismatch and demonstrate a real idea-to-outputs example.
2. Design one complete first-post journey, including missing media, unconnected destinations and revision.
3. Establish the persistent Studio canvas, focused mobile modes and contextual controls.
4. Make voice and platform adaptation visible, correctable and reversible.
5. Integrate destination readiness, preview, approval and outcomes into the same named piece of work.
6. Apply a unified type, spacing, icon and component system, preserving Zova's identity.
7. Test the full journey with target creators and iterate on observed failures.

Proposed acceptance targets—not observed results: at least 8 of 10 recruited target creators produce and find a saved draft without coaching; all can accurately identify the account, content and timing before confirming; none lose their work during the tested recovery tasks; most can explain why the platform versions differ and recognise their own voice. Measure median time to a useful draft against today's baseline and test whether Zova is preferred to their existing workflow. A small formative study can reveal problems; it cannot establish market leadership on its own.

No new production actions, provider calls, branding replacement or UI implementation were performed as part of this assessment.
